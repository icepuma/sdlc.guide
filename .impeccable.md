## Design Context

### Users

Mixed software teams: product managers, designers, engineers, technical leads, and people who are new to formal delivery practices. They use the guide during planning and design work to understand what a lifecycle tool is for, what must enter it, and what it must hand to the next part of the lifecycle.

### Brand Personality

Fresh, clear, rhythmic. The site should feel optimistic without becoming childish or noisy.

### Aesthetic Direction

A kinetic editorial dial on a calm cream or deep-plum field. Seven interlocking arrow segments form the lifecycle ring, with curved phase labels set into the band and a central focus lens. A compact guide index and three uniform guide pages extend the wheel without competing with it. The fresh editorial palette carries the mood without literal imagery. Funnel Sans carries all text with a clear, rhythmic voice for small labels and comfortable long-form reading. Light and dark themes are designed as peers. Avoid generic infographic donuts, floating cards, illustrative metaphors, glass panels, neon effects, and heavy 3D engines.

### Design Principles

1. The wheel is the primary entry point; the guide index and guide pages continue its visual system.
2. Phase name, action, availability, and tools must be readable at a glance.
3. Color groups phases but never carries meaning alone.
4. Every wheel control keeps a 44-pixel target and keyboard order.
5. Theme and wheel motion are optional enhancements; the static page stays complete.
