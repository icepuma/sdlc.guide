## Purpose and boundary

Implementation turns approved design into reviewed code, records, and a candidate. Material changes need review. A green build is not readiness; [Testing](/verify/) gathers evidence.

### Flow

Use scope, criteria, and [Design](/design/). Material proposals need an [RFC](/design/rfc/); durable decisions an [ADR](/design/adr/). Give [Testing](/verify/) a versioned candidate with evidence, migrations, flags, and manual checks.

## What happens here

Trace small changes to requirements or decisions. Version code, configuration, manifests, flags, schemas, policies, and runbooks together.

Use [SLSA’s build requirements](https://slsa.dev/spec/v1.2/build-requirements) for repeatable builds. Record each source, dependency, CI action, base image, and tool with name/URI and resolved digest; a digest identifies content, not trust. Accept approved publisher or canonical source, immutable identity, and signature; reject mutable labels, branches, and `latest`. Centralise hardened definitions.

Keep secrets out of source, logs, definitions, attestations, and hand-offs. Untrusted pull-request jobs get no secrets or privileged credentials. Privileged work uses a fresh, isolated build from the protected revision and independently verifies pull-request code, artefacts, caches, workspace, and workflow values. Use short-lived least-privilege credentials. Before signing, a protected control plane generates or verifies provenance from authenticated records and controlled output storage; keys stay unavailable to user-controlled steps.

Record a bill of materials: URI, digest, reason, and gaps. Test rules, errors, conversions, permissions, components, and interactions in CI and locally; this finds regressions, not integration evidence.

Authors state intent, risk, and evidence; reviewers assess design, function, tests, docs, and coverage. Approval covers the [final protected revision](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/reference/security/secure-use); changes need review. Use specialists for material security, privacy, concurrency, or accessibility. Resolve or justify comments.

Keep integrity, provenance, test evidence, and reproducibility separate. Integrity compares a candidate with trusted digest or approved-identity signature. Trust [SLSA provenance](https://slsa.dev/spec/v1.2/verifying-artifacts) only after policy verifies its signed envelope against configured root; minimum accepted builder assurance/isolation and mode-specific identity (reject lower modes); allowed `predicateType`, subject digest, signer-builder pair, expected `buildType`, complete allowlisted `externalParameters` (reject extras/mismatches), and immutable source/configuration dependencies. `internalParameters` are not caller inputs; retain non-secret output-affecting values in protected, integrity-bound rebuild/incident records and exclude/redact secrets. Provenance binds recorded subjects, dependencies, definition, and builder—not hidden inputs, tests, or unrecorded migration. A migration is bound only when its exact digest is a verified input or subject; otherwise bind it in the authenticated image-bound manifest. Reject missing build-contract dependencies and record accepted gaps.

Accept approved versioned predicates or signed subject-bound evidence with a policy schema. A protected, versioned test policy pins role/digest pairs for harness, test plan (required IDs), test/candidate configuration, fixtures/data, and target platform/environment. Evidence binds all named roles and the exact candidate subject. Policy checks envelope/root, trusted signer-runner pair, and that every role/digest equals the authenticated image-bound manifest or test policy; URLs never count. Require [in-toto Test Result evidence](https://github.com/in-toto/attestation/blob/v1.2.0/spec/predicates/test-result.md): `PASSED`, required IDs in `passedTests`, no `failedTests`, and policy-accepted warnings. A source-revision fallback must identify the canonical repository and match the immutable revision in the candidate's verified provenance dependency; otherwise use exact subject.

[Reproducibility](https://reproducible-builds.org/docs/plans/) means specified unsigned primary subjects rebuild byte-identically from declared inputs, platform, environment, and instructions; logs are outside them. Embedded-signature transforms are versioned, documented, scripted, audited, and proved limited to safe signature bytes; otherwise include signatures. Use independently controlled rebuilds; classify mismatches with owner/reason.

### Who owns what

Name risk and blocker owners. Engineers maintain evidence; reviewers approve work. Product confirms outcomes; design, security, and operations check accessibility, boundaries/secrets, and recovery.

## Inputs and outputs

Start with scope, criteria, design, constraints, checks, and invariants. Leave with reviewed source/configuration, unit evidence, repeatable build, and trust records linked to change, design, migrations, and checks.

### Continue when

- Behaviour traces to approved requirements and design decisions.
- Code, configuration, and documentation have risk-proportionate review of the final protected revision; concerns are resolved or accepted.
- Build, static checks, and relevant tests pass for the exact source revision and candidate.
- Every artefact input has URI and resolved digest. Each non-secret scalar external parameter has exact typed allowlisted value or content-addressed configuration. No hidden output value is allowed unless an access-controlled, integrity-bound record retains every non-secret output-affecting internal value for rebuild/incident use; actual secrets are excluded/redacted and never reach untrusted jobs. Privileged builds are fresh protected-revision builds independently verifying pull-request inputs.
- A protected control plane writes signed provenance from authenticated records and controlled output storage. Policy verifies envelope/root, minimum assurance/isolation level, mode-specific builder identity, type, subject, signer-builder pair, build type, complete allowlisted `externalParameters`, source/configuration digests, and dependencies; it rejects extras/mismatches, lower modes, and unrecorded migrations as bound.
- Test policy accepts only that evidence. It checks root/signer-runner, every named role against the authenticated image-bound manifest or protected test policy, `PASSED`, required `passedTests`, no `failedTests`, and accepted warnings; only explicit, documented non-behavioural exceptions pass. A source fallback equals the canonical repository and immutable revision in the candidate's verified provenance dependency; otherwise use exact subject.
- An immutable, authenticated image-bound manifest binds image subject (digest/platform/media type), configuration content or digest, flag rules/targets/values, migration digests, and toolchain descriptors. Accept it only through a signature to the configured trust root or a verifier-pinned registry-policy binding whose root and policy are recorded; verify that binding and every entry. Hand it to Testing with attestation locations/digests and results.
- Rebuild unsigned primary subjects byte-identically from declared inputs, platform, environment, and instructions; exclude logs. Embedded-signature transform is versioned, documented, scripted, audited, and limited to safe signature bytes; otherwise include signatures. Independently controlled rebuilds classify mismatches and own residual risk.

## Common mistakes

Do not code from a ticket title or add material decisions without an [RFC](/design/rfc/) or [ADR](/design/adr/). Do not hide configuration, scripts, failures, or uncertain integration. Avoid unpinned inputs, secrets, and mutable artefact labels.

## Illustrative guest checkout

*This fictional example uses invented names, figures, dates, and thresholds.*

Shoppers buy without accounts; account checkout stays stable.

Engineers implement the approved flow, migration, and test configuration. Tests cover contact/delivery, account checkout, duplicate submission, and unverified lookup. Guest support uses only the approved identity-verification procedure (IVP) and its IVP-issued, server-verified, single-use proof; another method needs a Design/PRD change. Before disclosure, one conditional, serialisable trusted-service operation validates the proof's approved issuer and integrity; binds actor, verified subject, exact order, purpose, issue/expiry, nonce, and issuance authorisation/revocation generation; atomically checks current function/data/tenant authority and equality of issuance and current authorisation/revocation generations, consumes proof, appends audit record, and returns least fields. Revocation and regrant writes share its serialisation domain and win: no consumption or data. An object ID is never authority. Tests reject forged/malformed, wrong actor/subject/order/purpose/tenant, replayed, expired, revoked, removed-access, concurrent same-proof, revoke/authority-change-versus-consume, and revoke-then-regrant old-proof requests.

The build produces an immutable image: digest, platform, media type. Its immutable, authenticated image-bound manifest binds that image, exact configuration content or digest, flag rules/targets/values, migration digests, and toolchain descriptors. Testing accepts only evidence whose named roles match that manifest or the protected test policy, apart from explicit documented non-behavioural exceptions. The [Testing](/verify/) hand-off includes manifest, attestation locations/digests, results, and test payment/email-sandbox references. Testing proves guest/account journeys, failures, accessibility, and analytics.
