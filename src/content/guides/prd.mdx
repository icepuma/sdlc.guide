## Problem and evidence

An eligible new visitor is a visitor whose trusted checkout-service `first_seen_at` was within the last 30 days and who has no known returning-account identity before enrolment, no authenticated account session, and no staff or test-traffic status. All of these conditions apply together at `checkout_started`; no later shorthand weakens the 30-day rule. Persist an immutable eligibility record for every start: `eligibility_verdict`, `rule_version`, `evaluated_at`, privacy-safe `reason_evidence_class`, and `crossover_state = none`. A later voluntary sign-in appends an immutable crossover record with `crossover_state = voluntary_sign_in`; it never rewrites the original verdict, evidence class, or assignment.

A known returning-account customer has an authenticated session or a server-side account identity available before enrolment. They remain on the unchanged account path and are never enrolled. Their `checkout_started` event records immutable `account_started_at = occurred_at` from the trusted checkout-service clock; neither a retry nor late receipt can alter it. An unknown logged-out or cleared-browser visitor is eligible only when every eligible-new-visitor condition also holds, including `first_seen_at` within 30 days. An unknown visitor whose valid trusted `first_seen_at` is earlier than `checkout_started.occurred_at - 30 × 24h` is not enrolled and remains on the unchanged existing flow; a value exactly at that boundary is eligible. Do not search for an account from a guest email address or device signal solely to exclude the visitor. If an enrolled visitor voluntarily signs in, keep the original attempt and assignment, append the crossover state and `checkout_mode`, and continue on the unchanged account path.

For this rule, `checkout_started` is its trusted checkout-service UTC `occurred_at`. An unauthenticated visitor is unknown only after the authoritative account-identity lookup conclusively returns no existing account. Its trusted `first_seen_at` must be valid and fall in the inclusive UTC interval `[checkout_started.occurred_at - 30 × 24h, checkout_started.occurred_at]`. A missing, invalid, future, ambiguous, or error/timeout result from that lookup or timestamp check is reason-coded `non_eligible`, does not create an enrolment or assignment, and stays on the unchanged existing flow. This safe handling does not permit an email or device lookup solely to exclude a visitor.

A checkout attempt starts at `checkout_started` and receives one server-generated, globally unique, immutable `checkout_attempt_id`. Its immutable `primary_started_at` is that event's trusted checkout-service UTC `occurred_at`; neither a retry nor late receipt can change it. It may create zero or one `order_finalised` `merchant_order_id`; the one final event records immutable `order_finalised_at = occurred_at`. Every finalised order maps to exactly one attempt; a new purchase is a new attempt. Carry the attempt ID through payment, order, support, fraud, recovery, and terminal events. Do not use `analytics_visitor_id` as a conversion join.

Before an experiment opens, its registry atomically creates a new `experiment_id` under a unique constraint. On a collision, the service rejects the create request, records the rejection, generates a new candidate, and retries; it never reuses or replaces an existing ID. `experiment_id` is exactly 36 lower-case ASCII bytes and must full-match this lower-case profile of the [RFC 9562 UUIDv4 layout](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9562.html) (version 4 and 10xx variant): `^[0-9a-f]{8}-[0-9a-f]{4}-4[0-9a-f]{3}-[89ab][0-9a-f]{3}-[0-9a-f]{12}$`. Before an attempt, the release service, not the browser, atomically gets or creates exactly one reservation for `(experiment_id, anonymous_session_binding)`. If that binding already has a reservation, a concurrent or reissue request returns its same still-valid authenticated, expiry-controlled opaque `rollout_handle`; an expired, revoked, or otherwise unusable reservation rejects rather than creating a replacement. Only when no reservation exists does the service generate exactly 16 cryptographically random `rollout_key` octets and a new handle. Before returning it, the service atomically reserves the immutable one-to-one `rollout_key` ↔ `rollout_handle` mapping with unique constraints on each value and on `(experiment_id, anonymous_session_binding)`. At first enrolment, it atomically adds the per-experiment opaque `analysis_unit_id` with unique constraints on `(experiment_id, rollout_key)` and `(experiment_id, analysis_unit_id)`; no primary record may share, merge, or rebind any of these identities. On a raw-key, handle, or analysis-unit collision, the service records the collision, rolls back only the incomplete contender reservation, regenerates the colliding generated value, and retries; an unknown, expired, revoked, or client-supplied handle rejects rather than creating or rebinding a record. A client carries only its handle, which resolves server-side to its reserved octets; the client never carries the raw key. The handle does not substitute for an account or visitor ID. The raw key remains only in the allocation service; analytics receives `analysis_unit_id` instead. Its exact allocation input is the octet sequence `ASCII("checkout-allocation-v1") || 0x00 || uint16_be(36) || ASCII(experiment_id) || uint16_be(16) || rollout_key_octets`, where `ASCII("checkout-allocation-v1")` is 22 octets, `0x00` is one literal NUL octet outside that ASCII string, each length is a byte count, and `||` means concatenation. The versioned allocation contract is `assignment_bucket = uint64_be(first_8_octets(HMAC-SHA-256(experiment_secret[secret_version], allocation_input))) mod 10,000`. [NIST FIPS 198-1](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/198-1/final) defines HMAC over an exact message sequence. The experiment secret and its `secret_version` are immutable for that experiment. `allocation_hash_version` identifies this exact encoding; `allocation_version` records only the threshold band and is never an HMAC input. Persist the calculated bucket and arm with the first enrolment, publish non-production golden vectors with the full allocation-input hex, test secret version/key, HMAC output, first eight octets, and expected bucket; include a valid 36-byte UUIDv4 input, wrong-length/case/version/variant rejection, experiment-ID collision rejection, and forced raw-key, handle, and analysis-unit collision/retry or reject cases, and test them in QA-109. A reset of the key, secret, or allocation contract starts a registry-created new `experiment_id`; no existing experiment key is silently reassigned.

**Experiment-ID collision rule.** On a unique-constraint collision, the allocation service rejects the colliding create request and records that rejection. Only then does it generate a new candidate and retry creation; it never reuses or silently replaces an existing experiment ID.

At issue, the allocation service binds each `rollout_handle` to one opaque anonymous checkout-session binding, the `checkout-enrolment` audience, the allocation-service issuer, and its immutable expiry. The same atomic reservation makes that `(experiment_id, anonymous_session_binding)` unique, so concurrent issue or reissue cannot create another raw key, handle, analysis unit, or `N`. At every redemption it authenticates that session binding and verifies issuer, audience, and expiry before resolving the handle. Here, `client-supplied` means forged, unissued, copied, wrong-session, wrong-audience, wrong-issuer, expired, or revoked; each rejects before allocation or record lookup. A normal browser carries only the session-bound opaque handle in the protected anonymous session; the browser never carries the raw key. The handle does not substitute for an account or visitor ID. Analytics receives only `analysis_unit_id`.

At the first eligible enrolment, use the same serialised identity reservation to get or create the one primary record for the reserved handle, unique on `(experiment_id, rollout_key)`, `(experiment_id, analysis_unit_id)`, and `(experiment_id, primary_analysis_attempt_id)`, where `primary_analysis_attempt_id = checkout_attempt_id` for that first enrolment. In that atomic operation, record immutable server-UTC `enrolled_at` and select exactly one wave. For the current open wave, select it only when `primary_started_at ∈ [wave_start_at, open_checked_at)`, where `open_checked_at` is the trusted UTC instant read in the same serialised transaction; persist its frozen `retained_enrolment_rule_id`, not an unknown future `wave_end_exclusive` or `enrolment_retained_until`. For a closed prior wave, select it only when `primary_started_at ∈ [wave_start_at, wave_end_exclusive)` and `enrolled_at <= enrolment_retained_until`. Calculate the bucket from the selected wave and persist immutable `experiment_id`, `enrolment_wave_id`, `analysis_unit_id`, `primary_analysis_attempt_id`, `primary_started_at`, `assignment_bucket`, `rollout_assignment`, `enrolled_at`, `retained_enrolment_rule_id`, and `prior_exposure = false`; `wave_end_exclusive` and `enrolment_retained_until` are derived and stored only at the atomic close. The record is provisional until its selected wave is closed and membership-final: it cannot enter a closed-wave analytical `N`, frozen data set, registered look, or Release-gate decision until then. Wall-clock activity alone is not a selection rule: a current wave must satisfy its open interval and frozen rule reference, and a closed prior wave must satisfy both retained-validity conditions. A same-session same-handle retry of the same attempt reads the committed primary record. A request for an already mapped `(experiment_id, checkout_attempt_id)` with a different handle returns that original record only after authenticated proof of control of its still-valid original session-bound handle; otherwise it rejects without binding the contender handle. It never creates another primary record, rebinds an identity, changes membership or arm, or adds `N`. A later distinct `checkout_attempt_id` using that handle is allowed only in the same still-valid bound anonymous session; it reads the record, retains its arm, and persists a diagnostic event with its own `checkout_attempt_id`, the original record's `primary_analysis_attempt_id`, and `prior_exposure = true`; it cannot create another primary unit or add `N`. A copied or other-session handle rejects before this rule. Every primary eligible attempt has an append-only `primary_terminal_outcome` version stream. Its first reconciled event is `qualifying_in_window_order`, `later_order`, `zero_order_abandoned_aged_out`, or `zero_order_failed_aged_out`; every `qualifying_in_window_order` version binds its exact canonical `merchant_order_id`, active order-finalisation evidence version, and active `payment_confirmed` evidence version, while an abandoned or failed zero-order outcome records its explicit abandonment or reconciled failure evidence and aging. A late correction never rewrites that event. For each primary attempt, its stream has a durable active-event/version pointer. A correction reads that pointer and, in one compare-and-swap transaction that commits only if the pointer still matches the value read, appends a superseding terminal-outcome event with the prior active event ID, correction reason, trusted correction time, and any changed bound evidence version, then replaces the pointer. A concurrent loser commits neither change, then re-reads, re-evaluates, and retries. Each frozen snapshot records its chosen active event/version and its bound active order/payment evidence versions at `as_of_at`, so exactly one deterministic terminal outcome applies and a later correction cannot alter the retained old snapshot. Completion selects only a snapshot's active `qualifying_in_window_order` terminal outcome and those bound active evidence versions. Order and payment joins are required only when that active terminal path makes them applicable; never fabricate a missing order or payment for a legitimate zero-order outcome. A protective exposure override may suppress the treatment offer for that pinned handle or new enrolment, but never rewrites its assignment, membership, or evidence. Only one wave configuration may be wall-clock active for an experiment. Closing one and activating its successor is an atomic server-side cutover; frozen closed intervals remain selectable only through their registered retained-validity bound, so primary creation cannot cross a threshold version. Each wave analyses only its membership-final first-enrolment membership.

The same atomic primary-record write stores the selected **planned** `experience_id` and `experience_version` with the arm. A later diagnostic copies that same frozen planned arm and experience; neither can change, merge, or rebind for the primary unit. Only a linked append-only actual-delivery override can record a different delivered experience or configuration; it never changes the planned values.

At that same atomic enrolment, a current open wave is eligible only when `primary_started_at ∈ [wave_start_at, open_checked_at)`, where `open_checked_at` is the trusted UTC instant read in the same serialised transaction; it persists the wave's frozen `retained_enrolment_rule_id`, not an unknown future end or derived bound. A closed prior wave is eligible only when its frozen interval satisfies `primary_started_at ∈ [wave_start_at, wave_end_exclusive)` and `enrolled_at <= enrolment_retained_until`. The atomic cutover writes the closing wave's `wave_end_exclusive`, derives and stores its `enrolment_retained_until`, and opens its successor at that same trusted UTC instant. If an attempt began in a prior interval and its enrolment remains within that prior interval's retained-validity bound, its primary record retains that prior wave's frozen allocation configuration, threshold, and arm experience even after the successor becomes wall-clock active. If neither the current open-wave rule nor one closed prior-wave rule applies, reject enrolment and require a new checkout attempt. Never move an attempt's outcome interval across a cutover.

R9 treats eligible `checkout_started` records as three disjoint paths. A first-enrolment eligible start has `prior_exposure = false` and must reconcile to exactly one first enrolment, one `analysis_unit_id`, and one `primary_analysis_attempt_id`; it is the only path that can contribute `N`, and does so only after its wave is membership-final. A later eligible start using the same still-valid bound handle has `prior_exposure = true` and must reconcile to exactly one immutable diagnostic event linked to that existing primary record; it must not create an enrolment, analysis unit, primary attempt, membership, or `N`. An eligible start that selects a closed wave but reaches first enrolment after its retained-enrolment bound records `expired_non_enrolled`; it creates no enrolment, analysis unit, primary attempt, assignment, or `N`. The reconciliation proves exactly one of these three paths for every eligible start, overall and by arm for assigned paths. QA-109 proves first-enrolment, later-diagnostic, and expired-non-enrolment paths, including retry, concurrent, and restart cases.

QA-109 also proves cutover-crossing attempts: a current open-wave start is selected only from its `primary_started_at ∈ [wave_start_at, open_checked_at)` and frozen retained-rule reference; a pre-cutover start enrolled within its prior interval's retained-validity bound selects and retains that prior frozen interval even after its successor becomes wall-clock active; and an attempt for which neither rule applies, including after its prior interval expires, rejects and requires a new attempt. It proves that, after close, no primary record can place `primary_started_at` outside its recorded `[wave_start_at, wave_end_exclusive)` interval or `enrolled_at` after its recorded `enrolment_retained_until`, and that a delayed retained-valid record is excluded from closed-wave `N`, a data freeze, a registered look, and a gate until the retained bound and 100% eligible-start reconciliation both pass.

Before opening any wave, [STAT-1.0](https://example.invalid/analysis/STAT-1.0) registers its frozen `eligibility_rule_version`, UTC start rule, outcome-independent close rule, target distinct `analysis_unit_id`s per arm, maximum duration, closure authority, planned look, threshold `allocation_version`, and maximum retained-enrolment delay. At its atomic close, that delay derives and stores the immutable `enrolment_retained_until = wave_end_exclusive + retained-enrolment delay`; only after close may a start inside that frozen interval enrol by that derived bound. Closing freezes the interval and allocation configuration, not membership. Membership is provisional until `as_of_at >= enrolment_retained_until` and 100% of eligible starts for that interval reconcile through their registered first-enrolment, later-diagnostic, or expired-non-enrolment path. Only then is membership final and immutable. No closed-wave analytical `N`, frozen data set, registered look, or Release-gate decision may occur before `max(enrolment_retained_until, wave_end_exclusive + each applicable metric duration + watermark)` and that reconciliation. The close rule is: close only when every arm reaches its registered target, or when maximum duration expires, whichever occurs first; it never uses a comparative result. Only @checkout-sponsor may authorise a next wave. The sole Release gate may contain a sponsor-approved, bounded maintenance-rollover policy; it is the only standing authorisation for an automatic maintenance successor, and it names its immutable policy ID, exact permitted 98% / 2% successor, expiry, required live gate-state generation, and explicit remaining-successor budget. A material eligibility-rule change closes the wave and starts a newly registered experiment or wave; it cannot alter an open cohort.

For every wave, [STAT-1.0](https://example.invalid/analysis/STAT-1.0) must name immutable **planned** `guest_offer` and `control` `experience_id`/`experience_version` values. `guest_offer` is the named versioned guest-checkout experience. `control` is the named versioned unchanged existing forced-sign-in experience. An experience includes every user-visible flow, server behaviour, flag/configuration state, provider integration, and policy that can affect a registered outcome. The map covers all 10,000 buckets: a registered guest range receives `guest_offer`; every complementary range receives `control`. Persist the selected arm and planned experience ID/version with the primary record. Neither planned arm experience nor its version can change within a wave; a planned change closes that wave and starts a newly registered wave or experiment. A protective actual-delivery override is a recorded deviation for an affected unit or scope, not a new assignment map or a change to the planned experience.

**Retained-enrolment contract.** Before a wave opens, [STAT-1.0](https://example.invalid/analysis/STAT-1.0) locks a `retained_enrolment_rule_id`, its exact maximum delay, and the deterministic rule `enrolment_retained_until = wave_end_exclusive + retained_enrolment_delay`. While a wave is active, the wave configuration, reservation, and any primary enrolment select it from `primary_started_at ∈ [wave_start_at, open_checked_at)` and persist the selected wave and immutable rule reference, not an unknown future `wave_end_exclusive` or `enrolment_retained_until` timestamp. At its atomic close, the service writes `wave_end_exclusive`, derives and stores that one immutable timestamp from the locked rule, and opens the successor at the same trusted UTC instant. A start in the closing wave can enrol after cutover only when its `primary_started_at` is in that closed interval and its `enrolled_at` is no later than the derived bound. Membership is final only after that bound and 100% reconciliation of every eligible start, including the expired path below; no closed-wave `N`, frozen data set, look, or promotion gate may use it earlier.

**Expired non-enrolment.** If an eligible `checkout_started` selects a closed wave but reaches first enrolment after that wave's derived retained-enrolment bound, the service appends one immutable `expired_non_enrolled` reconciliation record. It carries the source event and `checkout_attempt_id`, selected `enrolment_wave_id` and `retained_enrolment_rule_id`, derived bound, detected time, and reason. It creates no enrolment, analysis unit, primary attempt, assignment, or `N`; it remains on the existing flow and requires a new checkout attempt. ## Intended outcome

Eligible new visitors can complete a purchase without creating an account. Known returning-account customers keep the unchanged account checkout. An unknown logged-out or cleared-browser visitor can use guest checkout only when their trusted `first_seen_at` is within 30 days and every other eligibility condition holds; otherwise they are not enrolled and stay on the existing flow. An enrolled visitor who voluntarily signs in crosses to the unchanged account path without changing their original record or assignment.

- User outcome · Complete a purchase without being forced to create an account.
- Business outcome · Increase intention-to-treat eligible-new-visitor checkout completion.
- Support outcome · Reduce `account-forced` support contacts without increasing harmonised order-support contacts.
- Safety outcome · Protect payment, privacy, accessibility, fraud, and service availability.

## Scope

At every frozen `as_of_at`, analytics records the CAS-selected active terminal-outcome event/version and selected active `primary_harm_outcome` for each primary attempt in both arms, plus the selected active fraud-harm/disposition version for every `logical_fraud_harm_id` used by that snapshot. A late fraud-harm, disposition, or per-primary-outcome correction uses the registered per-measure/primary serialised compare-and-swap transaction, which updates the affected logical pointer and derived primary-outcome pointer together. A concurrent loser commits neither change, then re-reads, re-evaluates, and retries. Neither path rewrites history. The old snapshot remains retained; the new snapshot uses the corrected active terminal and harm versions, invalidates any passed gate affected by the change, and follows the Release-gate correction action.

A registered early or long-tail fraud-harm-bound breach is a deterministic-stop signal and immediately pauses exposure through the Release gate. A pre-maturity `pending` outcome or pending watermark is not a harm-bound breach: it holds promotion or stage advance without an immediate protective pause. After the registered finality point, missing, unresolved, multi-mapped, or `incomplete` named-source evidence is a deterministic-stop condition; another non-harm review condition follows its registered holdback-or-stop action and cannot promote exposure.

Support attribution is never an `attempt or order` guess. A support event carries `checkout_attempt_id` when it is created. If it has only an order reference, the server resolves that reference to exactly one `merchant_order_id` and then one attempt. If both IDs are present they must match. A no-match, multi-match, or mismatch is excluded from the rate, reported as a data incident, and cannot infer an arm from a visitor ID.

**Payment-revision QA evidence.** QA-107 proves initial and every registered recovery revision has an immutable `payment_revision_id` plus its current canonical payload and risk-input digests; exact retry reuses that revision; and a stale revision's provider result has no effect. QA-108 proves that a changed payment method and another risk-relevant recovery revision atomically invalidate the earlier fraud decision, create the fresh active-revision/current-risk-digest `fraud_required` and `fraud_decision` pair, and block both provider-result finalisation and fulfilment while that pair is stale, missing, or mismatched. QA-109 proves the active-pointer and immutable revision/digest/decision joins across payment, fraud, payment confirmation, order finalisation, and fulfilment, and fails R9 deterministically if a stale revision or decision has effect.

**Payment-revision proof.** QA-108 proves that every applicable initial or recovery revision has a separate immutable `fraud_required`/`fraud_decision` chain and that each review or disposition keeps its originating decision, revision, policy version, payload digest, and risk-input digest. It proves that a changed payment method or another risk-relevant recovery revision invalidates the old chain, leaves later old-chain outcomes as audit-only evidence, and requires a matching active-revision chain before fulfilment; an identical digest on the old revision cannot satisfy the new revision. QA-109 proves the R9 uniqueness, active-pointer, left-join, and payment-to-fraud-to-confirmation-to-order-to-fulfilment reconciliation paths, including same-digest/different-revision, stale-revision, cross-revision, missing, and fan-out negative cases. Correctly retained historical evidence has no effect. Accepting it with authorising, payment, order-finalisation, or fulfilment effect is a deterministic R9 failure.

## Release gate (normative)

This table is the sole normative source for all release decisions. Metric owners detect and report conditions only; a registered deterministic-stop signal or gate-invalidating correction takes effect only through this table. Deterministic safety stops do not wait for sample size, maturity, a scheduled look, or @checkout-sponsor availability. @release-manager may only make a deterministic pause or apply the registered correction override; @checkout-sponsor alone controls go, stop, re-exposure, and 100%. A reduction preserves assignment evidence and allows already-started orders to reach a safe, reconciled outcome; it never forces an account or cancels a valid order.

The Before-2% gate inherits the non-waivable [Release](/release/) software-release prerequisite: the exact tested artefact, authenticated release manifest and provenance, and deployed controller/runtime readback must match and be retained. QA evidence for another subject, configuration, migration, or flag state cannot pass this gate.
