overview:
  paragraphs:
    - A product requirements document makes a proposed product change reviewable. This guide uses it to record the user problem and evidence, intended outcome, scope boundaries, expected behaviour, and measures.
    - A named record steward keeps the PRD current and links research, designs, constraints, risks, dependencies, and delivery work. A named decision authority uses the gate to choose go, hold, stop, recovery, or a scope change. Use an RFC when a technical trade-off needs its own proposal.
  takeaway: State the outcome, boundaries, evidence, and next decision. Link detailed technical and operational work instead of copying it.
  useWhen:
    - A product or feature needs shared intent across disciplines
    - The user problem, scope, or expected behaviour needs review
    - The team needs measurable outcomes and a clear review point
    - Assumptions, dependencies, or open questions could change the work
  skipWhen:
    - A contained maintenance change already has clear evidence and acceptance criteria
    - A low-risk, reversible experiment fits in a short brief or backlog item
    - The document would only repeat information that is current and reviewable elsewhere
  anatomy:
    - term: Problem and evidence
      description: Who is affected, what happens now, and the evidence behind the claim.
    - term: Outcome and scope
      description: The intended result, what is included, and what is deliberately excluded.
    - term: Users and requirements
      description: Stable user-need IDs, observable behaviour, and linked acceptance evidence.
    - term: Measures and decision gate
      description: The outcome, safety checks, owners, and the action each result supports.
    - term: Risks and dependencies
      description: Items that could affect the work, with an owner and gate consequence.
    - term: Delivery plan and open questions
      description: Checkpoints, proof, and decisions still needed before delivery.
  dos:
    - Name a record steward and a separate decision authority
    - Write it with product, design, engineering, research, and affected partners
    - Link each requirement to a user need and planned acceptance evidence
    - Define only the measures that inform a decision
    - Keep go, hold, stop, and recovery conditions in one decision-gate table
    - Link detailed designs, policies, and technical contracts instead of duplicating them
  donts:
    - Specify every implementation detail before the team has learned enough
    - Use sign-off as a reason to freeze requirements that should change
    - Treat delivery as success instead of defining an outcome for users or the business
    - Write the PRD alone or duplicate detailed work that can be linked
    - Use Must or Should without defining the release-priority rule
    - Treat a historical baseline as proof that a rollout caused an outcome

## Decision summary

Approve a 2% guest-checkout trial for eligible new visitors. Expansion needs registered evidence; otherwise account-first remains.

## Problem, outcome, scope, and constraints

The current journey requires an account before purchase. In the 12 complete UTC weeks from 2026-06-01 to 2026-08-23, 34% of eligible attempts reaching forced sign-in had no matching paid order within 24 hours; `account-forced` was the third-most-common validated support tag. Evidence: [CHECKOUT-18](https://example.invalid/research/CHECKOUT-18). This is an observed association, not proof that forced sign-in caused exit.

Eligible new visitors complete one purchase without an account, with no worse support, payment, fraud, privacy, accessibility, or service health.

In scope: a guest choice, contact and delivery details, approved-provider payment, confirmation, authorised support lookup, measurement, and recovery. Guest checkout creates no account, credential, reusable profile, or marketing consent. Order data is used only as approved for fulfilment, support, reconciliation, and retention. Social sign-in, loyalty points, post-purchase account creation, and changes to the existing account journey are out of scope. Constraints: the approved privacy, accessibility, payment-provider, fraud, and support records must remain current.

## Roles and needs

- **Record steward:** jonas@ keeps the PRD and evidence current.
- **Sponsor:** @checkout-sponsor authorises planned start, expansion, stop, and resumption.
- **Emergency pause:** @release-manager may stop new guest assignment for an affected scope. Existing attempts complete or enter the approved recovery path; only the sponsor may resume or expand.

| ID | User need | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| UN-1 | An eligible new visitor buys without creating an account. | [CHECKOUT-18](https://example.invalid/research/CHECKOUT-18) |
| UN-2 | A returning-account customer keeps the current journey. | [DES-204](https://example.invalid/design/DES-204) |
| UN-3 | A checkout user can review and correct the order before payment. | [ACC-08](https://example.invalid/accessibility/ACC-08) |
| UN-4 | A customer can recover from payment uncertainty without an unresolved second charge, duplicate order, or duplicate fulfilment. | [PAY-5.0](https://example.invalid/payment/PAY-5.0) |
| UN-5 | A support colleague can help only with the customer’s exact order. | [SUP-19](https://example.invalid/support/SUP-19) |

## Requirements

All six requirements are Must for this release.

| ID | Need | Observable behaviour and proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | UN-1, UN-2 | Guest checkout is offered only when the approved eligibility service classifies an unauthenticated visitor as eligible. Missing or ambiguous evidence keeps the current path. [QA-101](https://example.invalid/acceptance/QA-101) |
| R2 | UN-1 | The flow creates no account and uses only data approved in [PRIV-4.0](https://example.invalid/privacy/PRIV-4.0). Card data goes to the hosted provider; the service never stores PAN or CVV/CVC. [QA-102](https://example.invalid/acceptance/QA-102) |
| R3 | UN-3 | All organisation-controlled guest pages and states meet [WCAG 2.2 AA](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#conformance-reqs). The provider hand-off has approved accessibility evidence, tested hand-off, and documented recovery route. [QA-103](https://example.invalid/acceptance/QA-103) |
| R4 | UN-4 | A retry, lost response, callback, or query creates no duplicate order or fulfilment and leaves no unresolved second charge. The server verifies the matching provider result before finalisation; browser return is not proof. Follow [payment recovery](/plan/prd/payment-recovery/). [QA-104](https://example.invalid/acceptance/QA-104) |
| R5 | UN-5 | Support discloses one order only when the approved [exact-order verification record](https://example.invalid/support/SUP-19) establishes the requester’s entitlement. Failed, expired, replayed, cross-customer, or cross-tenant checks disclose nothing. [QA-105](https://example.invalid/acceptance/QA-105) |
| R6 | UN-1 | Each enrolled attempt records its assigned experience, outcome, safety signals, and data-quality evidence. Use a concurrent control that meets `STAT-1.0` causal-design conditions; history plans but does not establish causation. Follow [controlled rollout evidence](/plan/prd/controlled-rollouts/). [QA-106](https://example.invalid/acceptance/QA-106) |

## Measures and release gate

| Measure | Registered decision use |
| --- | --- |
| Eligible-visitor completion | Compare eligible attempts with one paid order in the defined window against the concurrent control. |
| `account-forced` contacts | Track validated contacts in the defined window after an eligible attempt. |
| Payment and fraud safety | Track duplicate charge or order, unresolved provider result, fraud-control bypass, and registered harm limits. |
| Existing-path, service, and data quality | Track returning-account completion, support, accessibility, errors, latency, assignment coverage, and reconciliation. |

[STAT-1.0](https://example.invalid/analysis/STAT-1.0) fixes population, assignment, windows, thresholds, data-quality checks, and action before 2% starts. It pre-registers outcome review for 14 days after final scheduled 25% enrolment, after the seven-day outcome window and reconciliation.

At every start, expansion, or resumption, the sponsor acts only if the applicable QA-101–QA-106 checks, specialist approvals, monitoring, recovery exercise, and every pre-registered `STAT-1.0` condition pass; there is no unresolved critical harm, recovery, or data-quality issue. Otherwise, hold or stop. The release manager pauses new assignment immediately for a safety breach, failed approval, duplicate charge or order, unauthorised disclosure, fraud-control bypass, or material service regression. The sponsor resumes only with fresh recovery evidence.

## Risks and open question

- **Payment uncertainty:** Payments reconciles unknown results; unresolved results hold the rollout.
- **Fraud or wrongful block:** Risk owns the policy, review path, and registered harm limit; a breach pauses new assignment.
- **OQ-1:** Support and security must approve and test the exact-order verification procedure by 2026-09-01. It blocks 2%.
