## Summary

Our 14 partner APIs are polled every 60 seconds; 98.6% are empty. This RFC proposes signed webhooks with one shared receipt and effect path.

Decision: approve a one-partner, seven-day parallel pilot. It is evidence only: no polling reduction, cutover, or scale. dan@ must separately approve cutover after reconciliation; scale needs 30 days of outcomes.

## Problem

Polling adds delay and cost. The change must not lose, duplicate, accept unverified, or apply stale effects.

## Goals and non-goals

- Reduce latency and empty calls without losing completeness.
- Authenticate delivery, retain evidence, and make retries safe.
- Reconcile, pause, and recover safely.
- Unqualified partners stay on polling; unsafe effects are excluded.

## Proposed design

### Partner contract and ingress

Each enabled partner has an HTTPS endpoint and versioned contract. iman@ records authenticated identity, event-ID scope, signing, replay, limits, source access, and ordering. rene@ approves it; lee@ owns the runbook and evidence. A partner without stable identity, replay, and backfill stays on polling.

The receiver creates no receipt or effect until it verifies the provider contract. It commits the receipt and outbox before `2xx`, and never makes a downstream call in the request path. The detailed ingress rules are in [signed webhook ingress](/design/rfc/signed-webhooks/).

### One receipt and effect path

Webhook, polling, and approved backfill establish the same `canonical_event_key`: `{partner, verified_source_account, provider_event_id}`. The contract defines a versioned fingerprint that is equal across paths; raw signed bytes remain the signature input. Transport is provenance, never key material.

One shared receipt is unique on that key and stores the first fingerprint. A matching fingerprint is a duplicate; a different one is quarantined. Its transaction creates or finds the shared logical effect: event key, effect type, target, and business version. All routes and retries use its recipient idempotency key. Polling is observation-only or uses this path. Backfill uses an authenticated source-query adapter, then this path.

### Recovery, ordering, and data

The worker retries only known transient failures. A timeout becomes `unknown`. Continue only on authoritative, final proof of no effect for the same recipient key; otherwise keep `unknown` for recovery.

For ordered work, the recipient must enforce a monotonic fence or irrevocable no-effect tombstone. Otherwise exclude it or make it safely commutative. Keep replay material separate from logs for the approved period. See [reliable event processing](/design/rfc/reliable-event-processing/).

### Acceptance tests

The automated suite must prove:

1. A valid signed event commits one receipt and outbox item before acknowledgement.
2. Invalid input creates neither receipt nor effect.
3. A cross-route duplicate creates one effect; a conflict is quarantined.
4. A post-commit crash loses no event or duplicates no effect.
5. A late or unknown result cannot regress or repeat an effect.
6. An omitted source event uses the shared backfill path.

## Alternatives and prior art

More frequent polling adds calls and still leaves polling delay. Partner-hosted queues are an option only where a provider offers one. The status quo keeps weak recovery evidence.

## Risks and mitigations

Webhooks add a public endpoint, bursts, duplicates, invalid signatures, and sensitive payloads. Rate-limit partners, reject unsupported input, test key changes, retain polling and reconciliation until cutover, and hold on mismatch or unknown effect.

## Rollout and operation

Deploy behind a per-partner flag. Before the pilot, record 30 UTC days of qualified-event retrieval calls: polling, reconciliation, and backfill.

Run one low-volume partner for seven days. Reconcile daily and at the end. The eligible population is every qualified provider event in the UTC pilot interval after documented source-publication lag. The event-to-effect map defines required effects; reconciliation compares it with shared receipt and effect records. Backfill missing events through the shared path and retain source access through the final check.

lee@ collects evidence. The pilot passes only if the six tests pass; the eligible population has zero missing or duplicate required effects; at least 100 real events and 10 of each type are checked; one authorised delivery completes end to end; and p95 receipt-commit latency is below two seconds. It is first byte to committed receipt for valid first webhook deliveries. Missing evidence, a mismatch, an unknown effect, or a missed limit is a hold.

If it passes, dan@ may record separate cutover authorisation with the polling change, rollback, evidence, and owner. A failed reconciliation or health check stops enables, restores or retains polling, and reconciles work.

For 30 UTC days after cutover, count the same call classes. Scale needs a 90% reduction against the baseline, complete reconciliation, and zero missing or duplicate effects. Only then may dan@ enable the next partner.

## Open questions

- Partner qualification: iman@, 2026-09-01; otherwise stay on polling.
- Effect idempotency and order: kai@, 2026-09-02; otherwise exclude.

## Decision outcome

In review. If accepted, this RFC authorises only the implementation needed for the recorded one-partner pilot, subject to normal security, change, and release approvals. It does not authorise cutover or scale.

When review closes, dan@ records the decision, date, rationale, material dissent, and follow-up links. A later RFC may supersede this one only after it is accepted; an ADR can be linked as context or a durable result, but cannot be superseded by this RFC.
