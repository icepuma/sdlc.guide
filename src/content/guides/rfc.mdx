## Summary

Our integrations poll 14 partner APIs every 60 seconds, and 98.6% of those calls return no new data. This RFC proposes signed webhooks, durable event storage, and a replayable queue.

Decision requested: approve a one-partner, seven-day parallel pilot. The pilot tests durable, replay-safe ingestion. The API-call outcome is measured only after cutover: for eligible, migrated partners, API calls should be at least 90% below their pre-cutover baseline over 30 calendar days. Polling stays enabled during the pilot. A passing pilot does not authorise cutover or another partner. After final pilot reconciliation, dan@ must record a separate pilot-partner cutover authorisation, including the precise polling change and rollback, before reducing polling. Only after the 30-day outcome gate may dan@ record a separate scale authorisation.

## Problem

New partner events can wait almost a minute before we see them. Most polling calls return no events. They consume rate-limit capacity, increase cost, and make delayed data harder to diagnose.

- Fourteen integrations poll once per minute.
- 98.6% of calls return no new events.
- Some partners already warn when we approach rate limits.
- A failed poll can delay data until the next successful poll.

## Goals and non-goals

This proposal covers event ingestion only for partners that support webhooks.

- Goal: Reduce ingestion latency and empty API calls while preserving event completeness.
- Goal: Provide durable receipt, replay-safe ingress, idempotent downstream effects, and operational visibility.
- Non-goal: Redesign downstream event processing or require every partner to adopt webhooks.
- Non-goal: Remove polling before a partner passes the parallel-run acceptance checks.

## Proposed design

### Recipient semantic-integrity rule

Before recipient lookup or effect, the recipient requires one of two proofs for every outbound HTTP request: a verified non-transforming end-to-end semantic path, with no unverified TLS termination or rewrite between dispatcher and recipient of a fingerprint input; or authenticated coverage of every pinned recipient-fingerprint input. The coverage profile names the HTTP method, required `https` scheme, canonical authority, verified recipient service identity and its verification source, intended tenant and its authenticated mapping, exact normalised resource, every allowlisted semantic header including the logical case-insensitive `idempotency-key` field, and the body or a named full-request or body digest. A resource alone is insufficient: canonical authority, verified recipient service identity, and intended tenant are separate required fingerprint inputs. The recipient derives them from the actual request, connection, and configured service mapping, then verifies their coverage, equality, and any named digest before lookup or effect. A trusted proxy is eligible for the non-transforming path only when it proves preservation of every fingerprint input; if it terminates TLS or rewrites authority, service identity, tenant, or resource, it supplies an authenticated assertion that the recipient independently verifies. No optional or unauthenticated-digest path is allowed.

The recipient contract pins the fingerprint's named collision-resistant algorithm, canonical byte form and component order, normalisation rules for scheme, authority, resource, tenant, and semantic headers, verified-service-identity source and mapping, body byte domain, output encoding, and version. It persists that definition and expected value with the work. The recipient recomputes the fingerprint from the actual received request and verified connection or proxy assertion and, for authenticated coverage, verifies the required coverage and named digest before lookup or effect. Later references to an authenticated transmitted digest mean this required proof, not an optional substitute. A field-name requirement refers to the logical HTTP field name, not its bytes or case: [HTTP field names are case-insensitive](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#section-5.1), so `Idempotency-Key` and `idempotency-key` are the same field, and [HTTP/2 requires field names to be lowercase on the wire](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9113.html#section-8.2). The receiver still rejects missing, multiple, or malformed logical-field occurrences and any scheme, authority, service-identity, tenant, resource, semantic-header, body, digest, or non-preserving proxy change.

The coverage profile also pins the expected caller principal and non-secret caller-authorisation binding: authentication scheme, intended tenant, effective scope, key or client-certificate version, and policy generation. Before any token lookup, in-progress or stored-result return, status return, or effect, the recipient derives the actual caller principal and binding from end-to-end client authentication and verifies equality with the pinned intent and covered fingerprint. If TLS terminates before the recipient, a named trusted proxy identity must supply a separately authenticated, integrity-covered caller assertion that binds those same values; the recipient verifies that assertion before lookup or effect. A stripped, replaced, or mutated authentication header, mTLS identity, caller assertion, or binding fails closed before any token lookup, result, status, or effect. A later policy generation is acceptable only when the documented retry-equivalence rule maps it to the same pinned binding; it must never create a new effect under the existing token.

The automated contract suite mutates every declared fingerprint input—method, scheme, canonical authority, verified service identity or proxy assertion, intended tenant, expected caller principal and caller-authorisation binding, normalised resource, each semantic header including `idempotency-key`, and body or digest—and proves rejection before recipient or idempotency lookup, status or outcome retrieval, or effect. It explicitly tests shared-proxy authority and route mutations, and first-attempt stripping or mutation of the authentication header, presented mTLS identity, and trusted-proxy caller assertion. It also proves that an HTTP/2 lowercase `idempotency-key` field is accepted as the one required logical field.

Each webhook-capable partner gets an HTTPS endpoint at `/webhooks/{partner}`. A named trusted ingress rejects every ambiguous or invalid HTTP message before it forwards the request, lets the receiver capture a raw body, or invokes a verifier: duplicate or conflicting `Content-Length`; a single `Content-Length` that differs from the received body octets in HTTP/1, the sum of HTTP/2 DATA `Data` fields excluding `Pad Length` and padding, or the sum of HTTP/3 DATA-frame payload lengths; any `Content-Length` with `Transfer-Encoding`; unsupported transfer coding; malformed chunking; and invalid connection framing. HTTP/2 full DATA-frame payload bytes, including `Pad Length` and padding, apply only to wire and flow-control budgets, never `Content-Length` or raw-body comparison. Before application-level header or body limits, it enforces pinned protocol-layer budgets. For HTTP/2, those include the HPACK dynamic-table limit, encoded and decoded field-block limits, decoder memory, CPU, and time limits, and maximum HPACK integer and string lengths. For HTTP/3, they include `SETTINGS_QPACK_MAX_TABLE_CAPACITY`, `SETTINGS_QPACK_BLOCKED_STREAMS`, encoded and decoded field-section limits, decoder memory, CPU, and time limits, and maximum QPACK integer and string lengths. It also pins ingress connection count, concurrent request-stream count, blocked-stream count, and per-connection in-flight field-block and body-buffer bytes. Before verification or body capture, it enforces pinned limits for request-line and target length, total header-section bytes, header count, each field name and value, signature-header bytes and permitted candidate count, trailer-section bytes and field count, chunk-extension bytes, and header/body read time. It enforces the raw-byte limit while streaming, including chunked input; it never trusts `Content-Length` as the limit by itself. A terminating proxy forwards one fully framed, normalised request only, with no extra request, trailer, or connection-framing data able to alter the body or a selector. The contract names its error map: an HTTP/1 framing ambiguity or invalid framing closes the connection; a completed HTTP/2 or HTTP/3 message error resets only that stream; HTTP/2 HPACK and HTTP/2 or HTTP/3 connection- or framing-level faults close the connection; an HTTP/3 request-stream QPACK integer or string implementation-limit failure uses a `QPACK_DECOMPRESSION_FAILED` stream error; and malformed QPACK field sections, invalid QPACK references, the QPACK blocked-stream limit, QPACK encoder/decoder-stream faults, and other connection-scoped QPACK faults close the connection. An ingress failure creates no receipt, replay material, or effect.

Inbound trailers are rejected by default. A partner contract may permit only explicitly named trailer fields with pinned grammar, multiplicity, byte limits, and purpose. The ingress, proxies, and framework keep those fields separate from the header section and never merge them. A trailer cannot provide, replace, or override framing, routing, parser or decoder selection, signature algorithm, key, contract selection, authentication, tenant mapping, or idempotency. If a partner needs a trailer digest or signature, the fixed, header-selected contract names its signed scope. The receiver buffers the full bounded raw body and trailer section, verifies that digest or signature before parsing, receipt, acknowledgement or `2xx`, or effect, and rejects an unknown, missing, duplicate, or malformed trailer field.

Before parsing, decoding, or normalising a request, the receiver preserves the exact raw request-body bytes and verifies the partner’s documented signature construction over its exact provider-defined signed input. It pins the accepted signature algorithm, version, header grammar, signed envelope fields, and any permitted multiple signature values during key overlap. The bounded signature-header parser rejects excess candidates before cryptographic work. For a MAC, it uses a constant-time comparison for every permitted candidate and rejects absent, malformed, or ambiguous duplicate signature headers rather than choosing one.

The inbound partner contract enumerates every parser- and handler-selecting input: the canonical inbound target tuple of exact method, required `https` scheme, canonical authority, equal `Host` and `:authority` values when both are present, and normalised path plus query; endpoint, route, schema, `Content-Type`, `Content-Encoding`, charset, multipart boundary, event-version selector, and framework decode mode. Each input is either inside the verified signed material or authenticated provider lookup, or is fixed by trusted endpoint configuration. A route or endpoint may select only a candidate contract until its signed or lookup binding is verified by the receiver. Reject a method, scheme, authority, `Host` or `:authority`, path, query, or selector mismatch before decompression or parsing; an unsigned route or header must not select a decoder, schema, event version, or handler. For every enabled decoder, enforce raw-byte and decoded-byte limits, maximum expansion ratio, nesting or multipart-part limit, memory limit, CPU limit, and time limit before data reaches a parser. Persist the chosen target tuple and selector values and their contract, schema, decoder, and handler versions with the initial receipt or material reference. The inbound contract suite mutates every target-tuple component and selector, including one valid raw-body signature with each altered value, and proves rejection before decompression, parsing, receipt, or effect creation; a valid signed compressed bomb must also be rejected by the decoder limits before parsing, receipt, or effect creation.

Each partner has a signature and replay contract. It states the accepted algorithm, key identifiers, key activation and retirement, signature grammar, and whether a signed timestamp is present. When it is present, the contract defines its field and unit, maximum accepted age, maximum future skew, trusted server clock and synchronisation check, clock-health and uncertainty predicate, degraded-clock behaviour, and the permanent-rejection response. The receiver permanently rejects an out-of-window timestamp only when its clock is healthy and its uncertainty interval lies wholly outside the accepted window. If the clock is unhealthy, unsynchronised, or its uncertainty overlaps the window, the receiver takes the retryable no-receipt, no-effect path rather than classifying the event as expired. A timestamp-less scheme is eligible only when its replay ledger remains effective until every signing key that could validate it is retired and no longer accepted. For every response class, the contract records the exact provider response, acknowledgement deadline, receiver budget that is enforced below that deadline, overload and timeout behaviour, and the provider's automatic and manual redelivery behaviour. A receiver never infers that a 4xx response prevents another delivery.

Each partner also has an identity and effect-ordering contract. It defines the unique receipt scope of `{partner_id, source_account_id, endpoint_id, provider_event_id}` and which fields are authenticated. The canonical source event ID, event type, account, endpoint identity, semantic-effect identity, and every sequence or version used for ordering must be inside the verified signed bytes, or be bound to those bytes by an authenticated provider lookup. A route or delivery header is not authenticated merely because the request body signature is valid. For each event type, the contract separately defines a semantic-effect identity, its ordering domain across event types and streams, a comparable authenticated order within that domain, and either a source sequence or version with a monotonic application rule, or an authoritative source refresh rule. For a source sequence or version rule, it also defines a source-contiguous release watermark. A later revision is not eligible merely because it is the earliest known work item: before release, the watermark must prove that every required predecessor is received and in an ordering-safe terminal state, or absent or superseded under an authoritative source snapshot. An authoritative replacement-state proof may replace that watermark only when it durably proves that the later state subsumes every required older effect. A distinct source event that represents the same business effect, and an older event that arrives after a newer one, must neither repeat nor regress that effect. A durable per-semantic-effect serialiser orders local work but cannot by itself fence an HTTP request that was delayed after a local lease check. For every order-sensitive external effect, the recipient must enforce a passed monotonic source-version fence with a conditional write, or expose a recipient-atomic, irrevocable no-effect tombstone for the older full recipient-observable idempotency composite of verified caller principal, intended tenant, operation, the authorisation binding carried by that older request, and idempotency token. That tombstone irrevocably blocks every future request using that composite. A dispatcher-local attempt generation cannot scope or identify it unless it is authenticated on wire and part of the recipient's matching rule. The serialiser atomically selects and reserves the earliest eligible source version or authoritative-refresh revision in its ordering domain with the work lease. It blocks newer work while any older item is `queued`, `leased`, `calling`, `unknown`, or `failed` with `retryable=true`; it permits newer work only after every older item is terminal `succeeded`, terminal `suppressed` with ordering evidence, or `permanent_failed` or operator-superseded with durable safe-to-skip ordering evidence proving that the later state subsumes the unapplied predecessor. For an older full recipient-observable idempotency composite without a passed recipient fence, those local predicates are necessary but insufficient: before the serialiser releases newer work, the recipient must atomically persist the irrevocable no-effect tombstone, and any stale first send using that composite must return no effect. A permanent `not_applied` result or operator disposition alone leaves later work blocked. A recipient that cannot enforce the fence or atomically persist that tombstone is ineligible for that effect. A partner must expose the same stable, source-authenticated canonical event ID in its webhook and polling paths, plus a source cursor contract for each provider-defined source stream. The stream key includes partner, source account, endpoint, and the provider's cursor scope; the contract states ordering, an authoritative membership generation, pagination boundaries, inclusive or exclusive starts, maximum permitted rewind and backfill, retention, and maximum lateness. Before a stream, endpoint, subscription, or cursor scope is retired, the contract captures its retirement boundary, retains authorised source access through its finality bounds, and reconciles it through that boundary before calling it drained. Partners that do not meet those conditions remain on polling and cannot use this fallback design.

- Digest identity: every receipt `payload_hash` stores its digest-definition version, named collision-resistant algorithm (`SHA-256` by default), byte domain (`raw-request-body` or a named canonical byte sequence), canonicaliser and version when canonical bytes are used, and digest encoding. Duplicate, promotion, recovery, and replay checks use the stored definition and value. A change to the algorithm, byte domain, canonicaliser, or encoding requires an explicit audited migration; it never silently reinterprets a stored digest.

- Outbound-request identity: before an effect is eligible, construct and persist an immutable, versioned local `outbound_request_intent` with its digest definition and value. Its canonical byte sequence includes canonical recipient identity, required `https` scheme, canonical authority, verified recipient service identity and certificate-verification source, intended tenant and its authenticated mapping, recipient contract/API version, permitted proxy path, redirect and automatic-retry policy, expected non-secret caller-authorisation binding (authentication scheme, principal, intended tenant, effective scope, key or client-certificate version, and policy generation), HTTP method, normalised resource identifier, every allowlisted semantic header after its named normalisation, the body byte domain and encoding, exact body bytes or an embedded versioned body digest, and the immutable semantic operation with its source version or authoritative-refresh revision. This local digest governs the dispatcher; it is not automatically visible to, or verifiable by, the recipient.

  For each intent, generate an opaque idempotency token with a CSPRNG, documented minimum bit length, and fixed wire encoding. The recipient contract names exactly one on-wire idempotency field and its grammar and length—`Idempotency-Key` or a documented recipient equivalent—and the dispatcher sends one instance of that field, with its name and token unchanged, on the first call and every exact retry. The field name and token require end-to-end integrity, for example as required covered components of a message signature; alternatively, a named trusted hop must prove verbatim preservation. Any field-name, token, instance-count, authority, service-identity, tenant, route, or proxy change fails before recipient lookup or effect. The recipient validates the single field occurrence, grammar, and length; it rejects a missing, multiple, or malformed field without an effect and does not attempt to infer entropy from a received token.

  After verifying message integrity, but before any idempotency lookup, in-progress or stored-result return, status return, or effect, the recipient re-authenticates and authorises the current principal, intended tenant, operation, and scope under current policy. It derives the actual caller principal and non-secret caller-authorisation binding—authentication scheme, tenant, effective scope, key or client-certificate version, and policy generation—from end-to-end client authentication, or from a separately authenticated, integrity-covered caller assertion supplied by a named trusted TLS-terminating proxy. It verifies equality with the pinned intent and covered fingerprint before it can inspect token state. A missing, stripped, replaced, or mutated authentication header, mTLS identity, assertion, or binding is a no-effect rejection with no token-existence signal. It stores a non-secret, policy-derived authorisation binding and policy generation with the intent. The binding records the policy-defined principal, tenant, operation, and effective scope that govern result access and effect execution; it never stores credential bytes. The recipient uses a full composite of verified caller principal, intended tenant, operation, current authorisation binding, and idempotency token, and guards that namespace so an existing token under a non-equivalent binding cannot be treated as a new request. A completed outcome or an in-progress status is returned only when current policy classifies the current binding as exact or explicitly retry-equivalent to the stored binding. A revoked, narrower, cross-scope, or otherwise non-equivalent context is denied before a result, status, or key-existence signal is returned, and creates no effect. A policy-generation change cannot turn an existing token into a new effect: it permits only an explicitly retry-equivalent retry or a no-effect denial or conflict.

  Separately define a versioned recipient semantic fingerprint. The recipient recomputes it from the actual received HTTP method, required scheme, canonical authority, verified recipient service identity, intended tenant, normalised resource, recipient contract/API version, allowlisted semantic headers after named normalisation, and body bytes, byte domain, and encoding. If the contract sends an authenticated digest, the recipient verifies it and equality-checks it against its recomputed fingerprint; the transmitted digest is never a substitute for recomputation. It rejects a same-composite-key fingerprint mismatch without an effect. For a completed same-composite-key request with the same fingerprint and retry-equivalent authorisation binding, it returns only the stored original outcome; while that matching request is unfinished, it returns a durable no-effect outstanding/conflict result. A token in another principal, tenant, scope, or authorisation binding cannot return, reveal, or reuse this result. A changed local intent version, canonicaliser, recipient contract/API version, recipient identity or certificate-verification policy, canonical authority, verified service identity, intended tenant, proxy, redirect or retry policy, authentication or authorisation context, method, resource, semantic-header set, body byte domain, encoding, body, operation, or source revision is not a retry: it fails closed and requires a separately identified, operator-approved new-effect procedure after the ordering rule permits it, with a new token.

  Every later reference to a recipient semantic fingerprint, target, resource, idempotency lookup, status, or stored outcome is cumulative with this rule. It cannot omit canonical authority, verified service identity, intended tenant, expected caller principal, caller-authorisation binding, trusted-proxy caller assertion where TLS terminates, current authorisation, or the authorisation-binding retry-equivalence check.

- Before the receipt transaction commits, persist encrypted and minimised raw or canonical replay material, or create immutable durable material and verify its versioned reference. `payload_hash` is over the exact raw request-body bytes; a partner may use canonical bytes only when its canonicaliser and version are pinned with the receipt. Store the verified signature algorithm and version, key identifier, timestamp, signature, and other authenticated delivery-envelope metadata separately. A permitted delivery-envelope variation, such as a regenerated signature or timestamp, does not change `payload_hash` or turn the same scoped source event into a collision. Persist the selected parser and handler selector values, their contract, schema, decoder, canonicalisation or transform, and handler versions with every initial material reference. When material is outside the database, create it in durable `staged` state under a short, non-extendable staging TTL and a destroyable encryption key. At receipt commit, prove that the remaining staging TTL covers the defined promotion budget, including recovery and response margin; the receipt transaction deadline is strictly below that remaining lifetime. In the same receipt-and-outbox transaction, record a `committed-pending-promotion` reference with the verified receipt, its payload hash, scoped canonical event ID, semantic-effect identity, immutable semantic operation and source version or authoritative-refresh revision, full versioned local `outbound_request_intent` with its digest definition and value, the documented one-instance idempotency field and fixed-encoding CSPRNG token with its field-integrity rule, the expected recomputed recipient semantic fingerprint and any authenticated transmitted digest, and an outbox work item. A database uniqueness constraint or equivalent atomic conditional insert enforces the full documented receipt scope: concurrent matching deliveries create one receipt and one work item, while a same-scope mismatch follows the collision-quarantine path. A durable promotion controller retries a committed reference to `promoted` normal retention before the staging deadline and recovers that work after a crash. If staged material expires before promotion, atomically mark the receipt `expired-material`, keep its outbox unreleased, and alert and reconcile. Only an exact newly verified duplicate with the same scope and payload hash, or authoritative source recovery that verifies that same scope and hash, may attach a fresh `staged` reference and restart promotion; neither may create an effect before the fresh reference is `promoted`. The sweeper reaps a staged object only after an explicit abort or after it proves that no committed receipt reference exists and no in-flight transaction can still commit; it never reaps `committed-pending-promotion` material. Do not acknowledge, release an external effect, or return 2xx until the reference is `promoted`; if promotion cannot complete in the enforced receiver budget, return the partner's retryable response and keep recovery active. A matching duplicate returns success only when its scope and payload hash match and its reference is `promoted`; an `expired-material` receipt remains retryable without an effect until repaired and promoted. A collision with different authenticated body or canonical content creates a durable quarantine record with a promoted, minimised evidence reference and alert, and never creates an effect.

| Delivery condition | Receiver action | Provider-specific response and sender-retry assumption |
|---|---|---|

- Treat delivery as at least once and potentially out of order. The outbox worker has durable `queued`, `leased`, `calling`, `unknown`, `suppressed`, `succeeded`, `failed`, and `permanent_failed` states. `suppressed`, `succeeded`, and `permanent_failed` are terminal. A worker or reconciler may enter `permanent_failed` only with authoritative permanent `not_applied` proof and a durable permanent no-retry disposition. It is terminal for automatic processing: only an operator may record its disposition or approve recovery, and recovery creates a separately identified work item with an audit record rather than reopening the terminal item. Every recipient result is durably classified as `applied`, `not_applied`, `transient_failure`, `permanent_failure`, or `ambiguous`. A `transient_failure` is permitted only when the recipient contract proves that it did not apply an effect; otherwise it is `ambiguous`. A post-call `permanent_failure` may move to `permanent_failed` only when authoritative evidence proves permanently `not_applied` and records the permanent no-retry disposition; otherwise it is `ambiguous` and must reconcile. An authoritative `applied` result always moves to `succeeded`. `failed` holds only a known `transient_failure` or an authoritative `not_applied` result marked `retryable=true`, and is retryable only within its approved horizon. Each lease and outbound attempt has an owner, monotonically increasing fencing token, and deadline. Before the first call, it persists the full versioned local `outbound_request_intent` and digest, the named one-instance idempotency field and fixed-encoding CSPRNG token with its field-integrity rule, and the expected recomputed recipient semantic fingerprint and any authenticated transmitted digest. It reuses that token only for retries of the same local intent and recipient semantic request. A newer source version receives its own intent and token only after the event type’s ordering rule permits the newer operation; any changed stored intent is not a retry and follows the separately identified, operator-approved new-effect procedure. A semantic duplicate or an event made stale by that rule becomes terminal `suppressed` before an outbound call. Its durable record carries the winning semantic-effect identity, winning source sequence, version, or authoritative-refresh evidence, the comparison rule, and the suppression reason. A timeout, lost response, expired attempt, or other post-call result without authoritative `applied` or `not_applied` evidence is `ambiguous` and is reconciled with the recipient’s status endpoint or an operator before any later side-effecting call in that ordering domain, even when the recipient supports a monotonic fence.

Before the recipient may inspect an idempotency record, its current policy must re-authenticate and authorise the request and classify the binding as retry-equivalent; otherwise the dispatcher receives only a no-effect denial or conflict, with no result or status disclosure.

- The outbound dispatcher is a non-autonomously-retrying client. It makes only the exact `https` request in the local intent, including one instance of the named idempotency field and its fixed-encoding CSPRNG token, verifies the recipient service identity, and enforces the contract's end-to-end field integrity or explicitly trusted verbatim-preserving hop. It sends no cookies, accepts no semantic header or proxy mutation outside the intent, and does not follow redirects or perform HTTP, library, credential, or challenge retries. ## Alternatives and prior art

More frequent polling is simpler to build, but it increases request volume in proportion to the shorter interval and still delays detection by up to that interval. Partner-hosted queues would reduce work in the receiver, but only two of the fourteen partners offer them. The status quo keeps the current cost, latency, and operational blind spots. No relevant internal precedent was found.

## Risks and mitigations

Webhooks add a public endpoint and introduce requirements for signature verification, duplicate and unordered delivery, burst capacity, retention, and recovery procedures.

- Rate-limit each partner independently.
- Bound payload size before buffering the body, then reject unsupported authenticated event types without creating an effect.
- Use HTTPS. Rotate keys through a named, time-bounded overlap, and test normal rotation and emergency revocation before enabling a partner.
- Keep polling ready only where each provider-defined source stream can backfill from its own last contiguous reconciled position with the documented inclusive or exclusive overlap, and where the full permitted rewind remains within the receipt-ledger and recipient-idempotency horizons. Otherwise prohibit the recovery scan until an operator approves a new-effect procedure. Never derive rollback recovery from the highest observed webhook cursor.

## Rollout and operation

Enable the receiver behind a per-partner flag and migrate one partner at a time. Establish the per-partner API-call baseline before the pilot. Keep polling enabled during the pilot.

- Deploy the receiver, receipt and effect ledger, transactional outbox, replay queue, dashboards, signature-contract suite, and source-reconciliation job.
- Run polling and webhooks in parallel for one low-volume partner for seven days. Each day, take and retain an authoritative source-stream membership generation. For every stream in that generation, complete a source scan from that stream's last contiguous reconciled position through the documented overlap, prove its pagination or snapshot boundary, compare canonical IDs, backfill any missing event through the same receipt path, and advance that stream's contiguous position only after the result is complete. Before a subscription, membership, endpoint, or cursor-scope removal withdraws source access, capture the retiring stream's immutable retirement boundary. Retain authorised access, wait its finality bounds, and reconcile through that boundary before marking it drained. A change resets final evidence and starts a new candidate generation, but does not discard a retiring stream.
- Complete a final full reconciliation only after the later of the partner's documented maximum delivery lateness, automatic-redelivery window, manual-redelivery window, source-visibility window, and snapshot-completion bound. Freeze the subscription and source-stream membership at one authoritative generation, or re-list until that generation is stable; retain it and capture an immutable final boundary for every active stream, then complete every active stream through that boundary. Also complete the retirement drain for every stream retired since the candidate generation began. A change resets final evidence and restarts the candidate, but the retiring stream remains in scope until drained. Do not use timestamps alone as proof of completeness.
- Accept the pilot only when the signature-contract suite passes; every source scan, every active stream in the stable final membership generation, and every retiring stream since the candidate generation began are complete or drained; there are zero unreconciled canonical event IDs and zero unresolved, missing, or duplicate required business effects; at least 100 real canonical events and at least 10 of each required event type were reconciled; and one authorised, known source delivery was observed end to end. The configured receiver budget must be below every participating provider's acknowledgement deadline and pass overload and timeout tests. The p95 receiver-edge-arrival-to-durable-commit latency must be defined from at least 100 accepted events and be under two seconds. Any incomplete scan, membership change, undrained retired stream, missing stream, missing sample, undefined p95, unresolved, missing, or duplicate effect, or budget failure is a hold, not a pass. lee@ owns the evidence and dan@ records the pilot decision; that decision does not authorise cutover.
- After the final pilot reconciliation passes, and before reducing polling for that partner, dan@ must separately record a pilot-partner cutover authorisation. It names the partner, exact polling change, final-reconciliation evidence, rollback condition, and scope. A passing pilot alone does not authorise cutover.
- Test rollback by fencing new partner enables, retaining or deliberately retrying in-flight webhook delivery until the source subscription is changed, draining and reconciling the outbox, then polling each provider-defined source stream from its own last contiguous reconciled position with the agreed overlap. Resolve every `unknown` external effect, and confirm zero unreconciled IDs and zero duplicate business effects before the next partner is enabled.
- After a partner is cut over under that recorded authorisation, measure the API-call outcome for 30 calendar days: `reduction = 1 - (post-cutover eligible-partner API calls / equal-duration pre-cutover baseline calls)`, counting reconciliation and backfill calls in both periods. Before separate scale authorisation, complete a bounded final reconciliation of the full post-cutover interval for every active stream and every stream retired since its candidate generation began: after the same finality bounds, freeze the subscription and membership at one stable generation or re-list until stable, scan active streams from each stream's cutover boundary through its immutable final boundary with the documented overlap, and drain retired streams through their retirement boundaries. Prove zero unresolved, missing, or duplicate required business effects. This is a fail-closed scale-evidence gate owned by dan@: the reduction must be at least 90%, the API-call data must be complete, and the bounded post-cutover reconciliation must pass. If any condition fails, freeze all new partner enables, retain or re-enable polling through the verified rollback path, and require a recorded decision before resuming. If it passes, dan@ must separately record the next partner, scope, evidence, and authorisation before enabling that partner; pilot, cutover, or outcome success alone does not enable it. Retain the reconciliation and backfill path for each migrated partner.
